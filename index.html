01 // Discovery & Problem Definition

The Problem & The Constraints

In industrial automation—just like high-volume e-commerce—every minute of unplanned downtime translates directly to lost throughput and safety risk.

The Core Client Problem & Downtime Risk

User Pain

ADM initiated a massive strategic realignment by closing three legacy Midwest mills (including its historic Chicago facility) to centralize operations into a single, ground-up mega-plant in Mendota, IL. As the electrical project lead at the forefront of powering this facility, the challenge was high-stakes architecture design: constructing a fault-tolerant electrical backbone capable of supporting 3 million pounds of daily flour output without a single point of failure.

Cost of Downtime & Operational Risk

Consolidating three regional plants into one created an extreme reliability requirement. An unplanned electrical outage at Mendota would halt 60 semi-truckloads of daily distribution, creating immediate downstream supply chain failures for commercial bakeries nationwide.

End-User Personas (Plant Operators & Maintenance Techs)

Plant operators and maintenance technicians required modular, highly visible fault diagnostics and standardized control interfaces to safely troubleshoot 480/277/120 VAC and 24VDC systems without taking entire production lines offline.

PM LENS // Just like checkout downtime during Black Friday, a single point of failure in a centralized grain mill halts the entire revenue and distribution pipeline.

Hard Constraints & Validation Evidence

Specs & Proof

Delivering a fault-tolerant 480/277/120 VAC and 24VDC power and automation architecture for North America's largest flour mill meant operating within non-negotiable regulatory, spatial, and throughput boundaries.

Constraint 01 // Regulatory & Safety UL508A / NEC

Strict adherence to NEC industrial electrical installation focusing on class II Div 1-2 hazardous location codes and UL508A industrial electrical panel building shaped the footprint, ensuring safe operation amidst combustible grain dust.

Constraint 02 // Hardware & Lead Times Supply Chain / Footprint

Physical space limits in the electrical rooms and the power step-down equipment permanently suited to be installed outside dictated aggressive footprint optimization for the massive 3000A switchboards and VFD heat dissipation.

Constraint 03 // Evidence That It Mattered Baseline Metrics

The business model demanded 100% power efficacy and continuous 24/7/365 production across three milling units, a 110-car rail loop, inbound and outbound truck systems, and high-speed automated packaging systems.

COMPLIANCE // Zero tolerance for "move fast and break things" when 480VAC and combustible grain dust are involved—rigorous upfront discovery prevented costly field re-work.
02 // Execution & Iteration

The Build & Iteration (The Story)

How we took the ADM Flour Mill from system requirements to live commissioning across three distinct product phases.

01
Phase 01 // System Design & PRD

Scoping the Architecture

MCC / PLC / VFD Infrastructure

To balance ADM’s requirement for 24/7 uptime against tight capital budgets, I translated broad plant capacity goals into standardized, cost-effective Motor Control Center (MCC) and switchboard designs. Rather than relying on legacy single-source suppliers, I expanded our procurement network globally—identifying international gear manufacturers capable of meeting strict standards.

Key Architectural Trade-Off

I prioritized full physical redundancy strictly on critical core nodes while standardizing modular panel components across auxiliary drives, maintaining zero compromise on quality while optimizing maintenance overhead.

Definition of Done (Acceptance Criteria)

Definition of Done required comprehensive Revit MEP models of all electrical equipment in place, inspection of all mill equipment, ECAD schematic sign-offs, and rigorous point-to-point continuity checks before any panel left the shop floor.

SOFTWARE PM EQUIVALENT: Designing a scalable microservices architecture, defining API contracts between services (PLC ↔ VFDs ↔ SCADA), and writing a rigorous technical PRD before engineers write a single line of code.
02
Phase 02 // Agile Delivery & Execution

Cross-Functional Alignment

Agile / Scrum / Stakeholder Sync

Coordinating civil, structural, mechanical, pneumatic, and electrical workstreams required adapting core Scrum rituals to the physical job site. I instituted 15-minute daily cross-disciplinary standups and organized complex build phases into 2-week execution sprints focused on physical critical-path milestones.

Bridging Shop & Field Silos

Just as a software PM maps back-end API dependencies to front-end feature launches, I mapped physical trade interdependencies where concrete pours directly impacted conduit runs and pneumatic testing.

Sprint Rituals & Unblocking

Daily on-site blocker reviews ensured that spatial conflicts between civil foundations and high-voltage conduits were resolved before they became schedule-killing emergencies.

SOFTWARE PM EQUIVALENT: Aligning backend, frontend, QA, and DevOps teams through Scrum ceremonies—translating customer requirements into actionable tickets and unblocking cross-team dependencies.
03
Phase 03 // Incident Response & Iteration

Mistakes & Real-Time Pivots

Field Troubleshooting & Zero-Downtime Pivot

During commissioning, imported high-precision optical sorters—critical for final grain purity—experienced signal interference and voltage sensitivity when interfacing with local US low-voltage distribution feeds. With plant go-live at risk, I took immediate ownership by bridging cross-border engineering communications directly with the manufacturer's teams in Switzerland.

Root Cause & Triage Decision

By analyzing real-time electrical noise telemetry from the floor, we engineered on-site line isolation filtering and recalibrated firmware sensitivity parameters to match local power profiles.

Retrospective & Process Upgrade

This cross-border collaboration eliminated potential multi-week commissioning delays, ensuring 100% optical sorting precision right on schedule, and updated our baseline QA checklist for all imported 50Hz/60Hz sensitive equipment.

SOFTWARE PM EQUIVALENT: Leading a Sev-1 production incident war room—isolating the bug, shipping a hotfix with zero customer-facing downtime, and running a blameless post-mortem to harden CI/CD checks.
